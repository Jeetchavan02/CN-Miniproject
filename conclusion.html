Project Synthesis

Core Takeaways & Final Conclusions

Summary of empirical findings, real-world deployment trade-offs, experimental constraints, and citations.

01
⚡

QUIC Dramatically Reduces Handshake Latency

By merging the transport layer connection establishment and the cryptographic TLS 1.3 parameter exchange into a single round trip (1-RTT), initial connection delay drops by 75%–86% compared to TCP+TLS. Repeat connections leveraging 0-RTT send data immediately.

02
🛡️

Superior Resilience Under Loss & High RTT

Under 2% to 5% packet loss (characteristic of cellular 4G/5G and degraded Wi-Fi), HTTP/3 completes page loads 3x to 4x faster than HTTP/2. Because streams are multiplexed independently at the transport layer, loss in one stream never freezes unrelated streams.

03
⚖️

Modest Gains on Stable High-Speed Links

On pristine zero-loss fiber links with low round-trip times, HTTP/2 remains competitive and may slightly reduce CPU load. The primary motivation for upgrading to HTTP/3 is user experience on mobile devices and edge resilience.

Experimental & Academic Limitations

1. Browser Cross-Origin Timing Security

Browsers restrict exact sub-millisecond timestamps unless the remote origin sends explicit Timing-Allow-Origin: * headers to prevent cross-site side-channel timing attacks.

2. Single-Client Lab Testing

Our localized student tests evaluated single-client endpoints rather than massive multi-tenant ISP load testing or deep router queue buffering variations.

3. Corporate UDP Filtering (Middleboxes)

Certain institutional and legacy enterprise firewalls block outgoing UDP on port 443, forcing browsers to silently fall back to HTTP/2 over TCP.
